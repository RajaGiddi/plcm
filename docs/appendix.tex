\section{Instrument specifications and controls}
\label{app:instrument}

\subsection{The three-channel decomposition}

For each old task $k$ evaluated at the final parameters $\theta_T$ we measure
four accuracies and report three differences:
\begin{align}
  R(k)            &= \mathrm{acc}_{\text{refit,ceil}}(k) - \mathrm{acc}_{\text{ceil}}(k)
                     &&\text{instrument bias (recipe control)} \\
  F_{\mathrm{enc}}(k)  &= \mathrm{acc}_{\text{refit,ceil}}(k) - \mathrm{acc}_{\text{refit}}(k)
                     &&\text{encoder channel, recipe held fixed} \\
  F_{\mathrm{read}}(k) &= \mathrm{acc}_{\text{refit}}(k) - \mathrm{acc}_{\text{orig}}(k)
                     &&\text{reader channel, encoder held fixed} \\
  F_{\mathrm{tot}}(k)  &= \mathrm{acc}_{\text{ceil}}(k) - \mathrm{acc}_{\text{orig}}(k)
\end{align}
with the identity $F_{\mathrm{enc}} + F_{\mathrm{read}} - R = F_{\mathrm{tot}}$
holding by construction; the residual is printed per cell and was
$0.0\times10^{0}$ on every cell reported in this paper.
$\mathrm{acc}_{\text{ceil}}$ is the task's accuracy at its own boundary under the
deployed reader; $\mathrm{acc}_{\text{orig}}$ is the deployed reader at
$\theta_T$; the two refit quantities fit a fresh linear probe
(\S\ref{app:hyperparams}) to the frozen features of the era checkpoint and of
$\theta_T$ respectively. Measuring the refit \emph{at the ceiling too} converts
the recipe's advantage over an online-trained head from a bias into the
reported number $R$; the pooled $|R|$ is gated at $0.05$ per arm.

Reader share is $F_{\mathrm{read}}/(F_{\mathrm{enc}}+F_{\mathrm{read}})$ computed
\emph{per cell} and averaged (mean of ratios), never as a ratio of pooled means;
a denominator guard of $0.02$ is applied per cell and exclusions are counted.

\subsection{Capture-point identity (P3a) and its positive controls}

The instrument claims to read the \emph{deployed} readout path. That claim is
a premise, not a property, and is asserted on live inputs at the top of every
consuming script:
$\max_i \left|\,\mathrm{head}(f_i) - \ell_i\,\right| = 0$ exactly, where $f_i$ is
the captured feature and $\ell_i$ the deployed logit. The tensor captured is
architecture-specific:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Architecture & Deployed feature captured & Positive control (must fail) \\
\midrule
LSTM / MLP (PLCM) & post-composition cell state $c'_t$ & raw $c_t$ (pre-composition) \\
ViT-B/16 & post-norm CLS token, pre-head & pre-norm CLS token \\
ResNet-50 & post-global-average-pool, pre-fc (2048-d) & spatial \textsc{max} over the same layer \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Each control is a real tensor from the real forward, one layer or one reduction
away from the correct one, so the assertion fails on it only because the
capture point genuinely matters. Recorded control magnitudes,
$\max|\mathrm{head}(\text{wrong feature}) - \ell|$: LSTM (S72 OFF, $\theta_T$,
four tasks) $4.6$--$5.6$, flipping 8--33\% of predictions
(\texttt{runs/path\_identity\_controls.json}); ViT-B/16 $66.9$--$86.7$ across
five seeds (task-IL) and $40.3$--$111.2$ (class-IL), read from the
decomposition logs (\texttt{runs/e12\_decomp\_v2/logs/},
\texttt{runs/e12cildec\_*.log}); ResNet-50 $24.4$--$32.7$ across three seeds
(\texttt{runs/e14\_decomp/logs/}) and $0.246$ at the build gate
(\texttt{runs/e14\_build\_gate.json}). Live P3a read $0.0\times10^{0}$ on every
cell of every arm.

The LSTM-family instrument originally captured raw $c_t$ rather than the
composed $c'_t$ (catch 28); the correction is what produced the recompute in
\S\ref{app:results} and is retained in the process record.

\subsection{Selection semantics (P3b) and its control}

On task $k$ the readout module invoked must \emph{be} head $k$, verified by
object identity (\texttt{mod is task\_classifiers[str(k)]}), never by comparing
outputs---two heads can agree numerically on a batch and still be the wrong
module. Positive control: routing to the latest head instead of head $k$ must
fail the identity check. Both P3a and P3b controls were demonstrated before
first live use on each architecture. The E7 arm is the reason this assertion
exists: its heads existed and froze as designed, and no task was ever scored by
its own head ($\alpha_{\text{own}} = 0.165$; own head top-weighted in 0/24
cells).

\subsection{Label alignment (catch 32)}

Feature extraction collects $(x, y)$ in one pass per loader through
\texttt{channel\_decomp.load\_task\_data}. Two separate passes over a
\texttt{shuffle=True} loader draw two different permutations, and pairing the
second pass's features with the first pass's labels trains the probe on randomly
relabelled data; that defect produced $R \approx -0.63$ and, in E12, an inverted
conclusion that was retracted before ledger entry. Every decomposition in this
paper routes through the audited loader and asserts
\texttt{torch.equal(y\_tr, y\_tr4)} between passes.

\subsection{Era checkpoints}

One checkpoint per task boundary, fp16, \emph{audited by loading} at save time
through \texttt{PLCM.load\_era}, which restores the non-parameter
\texttt{task\_stats} that \texttt{state\_dict()} omits (catch 29: a bare reload
missed its own recorded matrix by up to $3.4$\,pp). The fidelity criterion is
reproduction of the recorded accuracy matrix, not successful loading. The
boundary-time value is authoritative; the fp16 reload delta is measured and must
sit below the arm's reproduction floor (ViT: $0.00000$; ResNet: max
$0.00400$, $27\times$ below its floor).

\subsection{Subspace lemma}

For the readout subspace $S$ defined as the row space of the classifier $W$,
$W(I - P_S) = 0$ identically, so any check of the form ``drift outside $S$ does
not move the logits'' passes by algebra. E4b's H-SUB3 was vacuous for this
reason and was struck (catch 25's first instance). Drift-energy fractions in
$S$ are reported against a chance baseline of $\mathrm{rank}(S)/d = 3.9\%$.

generated from the run seed; a checkpoint is always scored against its own
permutations (seed-1337 weights score chance against seed-42 permutations).
Full test split, no subsampling.

this paper is uniform on this axis, checked across the arms of the comparison.

\subsection{Probe fitting}
\label{app:probe}

All probes are multinomial logistic regressions fit with L-BFGS
\citep{liu1989limited} as implemented in \texttt{scikit-learn}
\citep{pedregosa2011scikit}, with $\ell_2$ regularization at $C = 1$, an
iteration cap of 5000, and the library's default tolerance of $10^{-4}$.
Features are standardized to zero mean and unit variance using training-split
statistics only, so $C = 1$ applies throughout to standardized features.

\paragraph{The default tolerance is not convergence, and we measure the gap.}
The default $10^{-4}$ terminates $5$--$20\times$ earlier than a tight fit and
leaves the objective $0.5$--$1.9$ units above its optimum; the iteration cap is
never approached, so it cannot witness this. Re-fitting the identical features
at $\mathrm{tol} = 10^{-10}$ gives the probe floor below, measured on every arm.

The floor is $\le 0.3$\,pp on the $256$-dimensional MNIST arms and reaches
$1.4$\,pp on the $2048$-dimensional ResNet features; HAR's $1.2$\,pp is
dominated by its test-set size, where one of 344--409 windows is already
$0.24$--$0.29$\,pp.

The induced error in $F_{\mathrm{enc}}$ is \emph{unsigned}. Across all 143 cells
it inflates $F_{\mathrm{enc}}$ in 57, deflates it in 59 and leaves it unchanged
in 27: both the era and the final refit terminate early, and which of the two
loses more decides the sign per cell. We therefore do not claim the direction is
conservative. No number in this paper is re-fit at the tighter tolerance; the
floor above is the quantity to read differences against. The
solver is multinomial across the supported library range ($\ge 1.2$); at 1.9.0
the \texttt{multi\_class} parameter has been removed and L-BFGS is multinomial
by construction for more than two classes.

Convergence is measured rather than assumed. Every fit is checked against the
iteration cap after solving. The guard fired on none of the 26 retained
task-incremental decomposition cells, evidenced by retained solver logs, and on
none of the 171 fits across the 57 class-incremental cells, evidenced by
per-fit iteration counts recorded in the artifacts. The most expensive fit in
the paper, a 100-way readout over all seen training splits, terminates at 564
iterations, 11\% of the cap. The guard detects iteration exhaustion rather than
abnormal termination before it; the latter is unlikely on standardized features
but is not excluded.

Probes were fit under \texttt{scikit-learn} 1.9.0, recorded per artifact from
this audit onward. The version behind earlier artifacts was not recorded, as
the execution image installs the library unpinned; those artifacts reproduce
under 1.9.0 to the single-sample level.

\subsection{Retractions}

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
\item \textbf{E12 ``encoder-side architecture boundary.''} Reported in-session
  from a probe trained on misaligned labels (catch 32): refit at chance,
  $F_{\mathrm{enc}} = 0.75$. Retracted before ledger entry; corrected values
  $F_{\mathrm{enc}} = 0.06$, reader share $91.8\%$.
\item \textbf{The tier-0 stack literal $0.347$.} Carried by citation with no

\item \textbf{``The H-C3$'$ instrument gate fails both arms.''} A symptom of the

\item \textbf{``Historical deltas are certainly code state.''} Generalized from
  one arm's zero floor to the pipeline; killed by a $1.44$\,pp same-seed spread

\item \textbf{``The nondeterminism is BLAS reduction order.''} Killed by HAR

  MNIST. Withdrawn; the observation survives, the mechanism does not.
\item \textbf{The adapter reference $0.9331$ and the attribution $+50.3$\,pp.}
  Averaged from runs recording no configuration, one of them
  (\texttt{fullrank\_ref}) later shown by measurement to be a different
  study's arm. Superseded by $0.9183$ and $+48.4$\,pp [$\pm 1.9$].

\subsection{The claim-integrity chain}

The paper's most-repeated sentence---reader dominance across four
architectures---was, in sequence: \emph{claimed} (from the summary sentence,
propagating by citation of prior copies); \emph{audited} when Figure~1 required
a number for every row and found no MLP decomposition existed; \emph{narrowed}
to three architectures the same day; \emph{measured} by E17 under a

narrowing stands); and \emph{restored} at $82.2\%$ [76.8, 87.4] (seeded re-run;

evidence then available.

\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}

unmeasurable, its arm being unbuildable at the sample size the prediction
named. A miss is a prediction
given above even odds that did not occur; a prediction given below even odds
that did not occur is recorded as ``did not fire'' and is not counted against
the series. The misses fall in three groups. Four ran toward more structure and less
determinism than predicted (E16's two LwF entries, E20-B's band and floor
pairs). Five ran toward \emph{less} encoder structure than predicted: E18's
lossy rotations cost the MLP 0.3\,pp where 5 was predicted; E21's one channel
transposition cost re-layout 14.5\,pp where 20 was predicted; and D1's drift
maps are neither near-orthogonal (spread 8--12 against a bar of 0.5) nor
linear (residual 41--45\% against 10\%), and on the pretrained arms a linear
drift model does not explain the frozen head's loss at all ($R^2 \le 0.06$).
Four, all in E23, are the largest single calibration event in this ledger:
every prior about the pretrained regime under a known map was transferred
from the scratch regime, and the data do not support the transfer --- on
pretrained trunks re-laying into the current frame recovers nothing and
costs, where on every scratch arm it removes the encoder term entirely.
E23-B then ruled out the alternative explanation (sequence length) with five
of six predictions firing, so the transfer that failed is the one across the
training regime and not across the length of the task sequence.
E21's
non-events (a crossing, a teacher advantage) point where every stored-self
and known-map result in this program has pointed: the author's priors
over-weight the stored self and under-weight the trivial use of the map. The E14 set was three
for three and uniformly under-confident, with the stated reason for doubt
empirically backwards. The E16 $F_{\mathrm{read}}$ miss is the most valuable
entry in the ledger: the prediction was wrong and the truth is the better paper.

A fourth group arrived with E25--E28 and is the largest single block in the
series: fifteen misses in thirty-five scored entries, a rate of 43\% against
21\% over everything before it. Seven are E26's, and they divide into two
causes that must not be pooled. \emph{Five are the regime}: E23's calibration
event recurring rather than a new one --- priors formed on scratch arms,
transferred to pretrained arms, failing again on where the frame sweep peaks,
and a held-out re-run registered at $\sim$85\% to test exactly that transfer
came back 9/18, exactly half. \emph{Two are the reader}, and they are a
different error: drift compensation was predicted to beat its own naive
baseline on both pretrained trunks, and the nearest-class-mean reader it is
built on cannot carry that much. Its \emph{oracle} --- perfect prototypes
recomputed from task-$k$ data --- reaches only 0.502 (ResNet-50) and 0.548
(ViT-B/16) where a linear refit on the same features reads 0.745 and 0.769,
so the whole prototype family sits roughly 25\,pp under the refit before any
compensation is applied. The prediction held on ResNet and failed on ViT, and
the prior that was wrong was about how much a class mean can read, not about
the regime it was read in. Two of E25's and two of E27's continue the D1 group:
drift repair is less linear, less composable and less near-equivariant than
predicted, on every arm where it has been measured. Four are E28's, and three
of those are one fact --- permutation augmentation costs more within-task
accuracy than predicted, at every $\mu$ tried. Two entries are misses whose
direction deserves recording without re-grading them: E28's ``B1 swapped
within 2\,pp of clean'' failed because swapped \emph{beat} clean, the opposite
of the failure the bar guarded, and its $\|A-I\|$ entry failed on a conjunct
($\delta$) that the measurement cannot resolve at $n=3$. Both are scored as
misses, because a bar is not moved after the data.

prediction held open indefinitely is counted by no one and quietly flatters
the series --- the same error as a miss recorded as ``did not fire.'' A
withdrawal states which experiment was not run; it is not a way to retire a
prediction whose experiment ran and disappointed.
